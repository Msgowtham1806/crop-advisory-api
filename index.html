What the records actually show

Findings from building this system. Several are unflattering, and they are published here because a decision support tool that hides its weak spots is not support.

FindingVariance

73% of yield variation is between districts, not between years

Soil, irrigation infrastructure, seed access and local practice decide most of the outcome. A model given only rainfall and temperature reaches R² 0.09; adding each district-crop's own historical baseline lifts it to 0.78. This is why the monsoon setting moves the risk score sharply but shifts the yield estimate only a little.

FindingCaution

The gain over simply repeating last year is modest

Repeating last year's yield scores R² 0.744 against the model's 0.776. The model earns its place because it does not need last year's figure — which a farmer planning ahead does not have — rather than because it is dramatically more accurate.

LimitationChickpea

Chickpea predictions are weak and should be treated with caution

Per-crop R² runs 0.70 for maize and 0.66 for rice, but only 0.13 for chickpea — worse than its own district-mean baseline. It is a rainfed rabi pulse whose year-to-year swings neither the baseline nor the weather features capture.

FindingEconomics

Most crops do not clear the full C2 cost

Measured against the full economic cost including imputed rent on land already owned, most district-crop combinations return a negative margin. That matches the cost-of-cultivation literature, and is the reason farmer groups argue minimum support prices should be set at 1.5× C2 rather than 1.5× A2+FL.

Data acknowledgement. This system is built on public records published by the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare, the Directorate of Economics and Statistics, and the ICRISAT District Level Database maintained with the Tata-Cornell Institute. We acknowledge the enumerators and statistical staff whose district-level collection over five decades makes work of this kind possible.

Independent project. KrishiDSS is an undergraduate capstone project. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or an official publication of any government department, and it is not individual agronomic advice.
